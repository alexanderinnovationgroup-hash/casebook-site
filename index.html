What “local-only” means here:

Your evidence files are sealed with SHA-256 fingerprints on your own machine. The app never sends your records anywhere. Questions

Asked by people in your chair.

Is this legal advice?

No. Steadfast organizes and preserves — it's a tool, like a binder that can't lose pages. The guide and statute flags are orientation written for self-represented people, not advice. Always check your local rules or talk to a lawyer when you can.

What happens to my case if I cancel?

Nothing. Your case is a folder on your device: sealed originals plus an index. It opens, verifies, and exports whether you're subscribed or not — the paid license only gates creating new cases and importing.

Can my ex get my records?

They're on your device, not a server — there's nothing to subpoena from us because we hold nothing. On your machine, the app lock and read-only "show to lawyer" mode control what anyone sees.

Does the sealed hash actually matter in court?

A SHA-256 fingerprint taken at import proves the file hasn't changed since — it's the same technique forensic examiners use. It doesn't guarantee admission (that's your judge and your rules of evidence), but it answers "how do we know this wasn't edited?"

Why does Windows warn me at install?

Unsigned apps get a SmartScreen prompt — it's about reputation, not a detected problem. Choose "More info → Run anyway." Code signing is on the roadmap; we ship builds as-is rather than make you wait.

Windows only?

Windows today, plus an Android companion for capture on the go. macOS, Linux and iOS are on the roadmap — the app is Electron + local files, so ports are a matter of build time.
